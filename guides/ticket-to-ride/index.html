How to score Ticket to Ride the USA base game

Most route points were scored when you claimed those routes during the game. At the end, count them again from the board so a missed or misremembered route does not change the result. Then add completed tickets, subtract unfinished tickets, and check the longest route bonus.

      When to count

      1. At the end of the game, gather your own pieces and cards. Check the plastic trains you placed on the USA map, your destination tickets, and whether you have the longest continuous route.
      2. Count route points from the board. These points are usually taken during play when a route is claimed. Recounting each route now is a useful check against a missed click or a mistaken total.
      3. Score every destination ticket. Add its value when its two cities are connected by your network. Subtract its value when they are not.
      4. Add the longest route bonus, then compare totals. Every player tied for the longest route gets the bonus.

      Route points

      Start with the plastic trains on the map.

      For each route you claimed, count how many train pieces long it is. Group routes by length, then score each group. The longer routes are worth more than the short ones.

      Where to look: your own plastic trains on the board.

      For example, two routes of four trains score seven points each, so that group is worth fourteen points.
      1 train → 1 point

      One short route adds one point.

      3 trains → 4 points

      Three trains on one route score four points, not three.

      6 trains → 15 points

      A six-train route is worth fifteen points.

      Destination tickets

      Take your destination tickets from your hand and check each one.

      A completed ticket adds the number printed on it. An unfinished ticket subtracts that same number. Do this one ticket at a time, then combine the pluses and minuses.

      Where to look: the destination cards you kept. Their value is printed on each card.

      A completed ticket worth twelve adds twelve points. An unfinished ticket worth nine subtracts nine, so together they change your score by three points.

      How do you know a ticket is completed? Find the two cities named on the ticket on the map. The ticket counts only if you can travel from one city to the other along a single unbroken chain of your own trains. The chain may twist and pass through other cities, and it doesn't have to be the shortest way, but there can be no gap in it and no route of another player in the middle. If the chain is broken anywhere, the ticket is not completed and its value is taken away.

      The longest route

      Check which player or players have the longest route.

      Each player tied for the longest route receives ten bonus points.

      Where to look: the trains each player placed on the map. The bonus is checked at the end.

      If two players each have a longest route of seven trains, both add ten points.

      How do you measure it? Each player looks for their longest unbroken line of their own trains and counts the train cars in it (cars, not routes and not cities). The line may pass through the same city more than once and may even make a loop, but each route can be counted only once. Branches that stick out to the side don't count: pick one way through and count only along it. Whoever has the most cars gets 10 points; if several players are tied, each of them gets 10.

      Common mistakes

      • Do not count the number of trains as the points. A route of three trains is worth four points, and one of six is worth fifteen.
      • Do not forget to subtract unfinished tickets. Their values can take points away.
      • Do not award the longest-route bonus to only one player when players tie for the longest route.
      • Do not count another player's trains as part of your route points.

      Who wins?

      Add route points, completed ticket values, subtract unfinished ticket values, and add any longest-route bonus. The player with the highest total wins. If players tie on points, compare how many destination tickets each completed; if still tied, compare who has the longest route. If that is still tied, they share the win.

      A whole game, counted

      Imagine your trains on the board show two 1-train routes, three 2-train routes, two 3-train routes, two 4-train routes, one 5-train route and one 6-train route.

      1. The 1-train routes score 2 points altogether.
      2. The 2-train routes score 6 points altogether: 3 routes, 2 points each.
      3. The 3-train routes score 8 points altogether: 2 routes, 4 points each.
      4. The 4-train routes score 14 points altogether: 2 routes, 7 points each.
      5. The 5-train route scores 10 points, and the 6-train route scores 15. Route points total 55.
      6. Your tickets are worth 12 and 8, both completed, plus 1 worth 9 that is unfinished. Add 20, subtract 9, and the tickets change the score by 11.
      7. You share the longest route, so add 10 points.

      55 route points, plus 11 from tickets, plus 10 for the longest route: 76 points.

      Questions players ask

      Do I count route points again if we scored them during play?

      Yes. The route points are normally scored when you claim routes, but recounting them at the end helps catch mistakes.

      What if a ticket is worth more than my route points?

      Score it anyway. An unfinished ticket subtracts its printed value and can leave you with a negative total.

      Can more than one player get the longest-route bonus?

      Yes. Every player tied for the longest route gets ten points.

      What happens if we tie on total points?

      The player with more completed destination tickets wins the tie. If still tied, compare longest routes; if those are tied too, share the win.

      Does this cover other maps or expansions?

      This page covers only the base game on the USA map. Other maps and expansions can use different scoring rules.

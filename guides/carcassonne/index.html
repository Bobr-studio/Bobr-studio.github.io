How to score Carcassonne the base game, step by step

In Carcassonne you score in two different moments. During the game, every road, city or monastery is scored the moment it's finished, and the meeples on it go home. When the last tile is down, everything that's still unfinished is scored for fewer points, and then come the fields, which confuse almost everyone. This page explains both, with examples, and the calculator counts the end of the game for you.

Your end-of-game count

After the last tile, look only at the unfinished roads, cities and monasteries where you have the most meeples (or are tied for the most), and at the fields where you have the most farmers. Add up the numbers across all of them.

    When to count

    1. During the game, the moment something is finished. When a tile closes a road, a city or a monastery, that feature is scored straight away, even if it's another player's turn. The player with the most meeples on it moves their marker along the scoring track, and then every meeple on that feature goes back to its owner, ready to be placed again.
    2. Nothing else is scored during the game. Unfinished features wait, and fields are never scored before the end. A farmer lying in a field stays there for the whole game.
    3. When the last tile has been placed, the game ends. Now look at every meeple still on the table. Each one is standing on something unfinished: a road, a city or a monastery. Score those, one feature at a time, but for fewer points than finished ones (cities and monasteries are worth less, roads stay the same).
    4. Fields come last. Once all unfinished features are counted, score the fields with farmers. A field earns points for the finished cities next to it, which is why you score it only when nothing can change anymore.
    It's easiest to clear the table as you go: score a feature, take its meeples off, move on to the next one. When only farmers are left, it's time for the fields.

    Who gets the points

    You can't put a meeple on a road, city or field that already has one, even your own. But two separate pieces with meeples on them can grow and join later, and then one feature has several meeples on it. When it's scored, count each player's meeples there. The player with the most gets all the points. If two or more players are tied for the most, each of them gets the full points, nobody splits anything. Players with fewer meeples get nothing. A feature with no meeples at all scores for nobody.

    Example: a finished city has 5 tiles and 2 shields, so it's worth 10 + 4 = 14 points. Anna has 2 meeples in it, Mike has 2, and you have 1. Anna and Mike each get 14 points. You get nothing, but your meeple still comes back.

    Roads

    What you count

    Follow the road from one end to the other and count the tiles it runs across. A road is finished when both ends stop: at a crossroads, at a city, at a monastery, or when it comes back around to meet itself in a loop.

    During the gameA finished road gives 1 point for each tile.

    At the endAn unfinished road also gives 1 point for each tile. Roads are the only feature worth the same either way.

    Where to look: the meeples standing on roads.

    Example: a road leaves a crossroads, runs over 2 plain tiles and ends at a city gate. Counting the crossroads tile and the city tile, it touches 4 tiles, so 4 points. If at the end of the game a road of 3 tiles still has an open end, it gives 3 points.

    Cities and shields

    What you count

    Count the tiles that have any part of the city on them, and then count the shields. A shield is the small coat-of-arms badge printed on some city tiles. A city is finished when its walls close all the way around with no gap and no open edge.

    During the gameA finished city gives 2 points for each tile and 2 more for each shield. The smallest city, 2 tiles with no shield, is worth 4.

    At the endAn unfinished city gives only 1 point for each tile and 1 for each shield, half of what it would have been worth finished.

    Where to look: the meeples standing inside city walls.

    Example: a finished city of 4 tiles with 1 shield gives 4 × 2 = 8 for the tiles and 2 for the shield, 10 points. An unfinished city of 3 tiles with 1 shield at the end of the game gives 3 + 1 = 4 points.

    Monasteries

    What you count

    A monastery sits in the middle of its tile. Look at the 8 spaces around it: left, right, above, below and the 4 corners. The monastery is finished when all 8 are filled with tiles.

    During the gameA finished monastery gives 9 points: 1 for the monastery tile and 1 for each of the 8 tiles around it.

    At the endAn unfinished monastery gives 1 point for the monastery tile and 1 for each tile around it that is there. So it's worth between 1 and 8 points.

    Where to look: the meeples on monasteries. Only one meeple can ever be on a monastery, so there's no sharing.

    Example: at the end of the game, your monastery has 5 tiles around it and 3 empty spaces. That's 1 + 5 = 6 points.

    Fields

    What you count

    A field is a stretch of green grass. It goes on across tiles for as long as the grass is connected; roads, city walls and the edge of the tiles on the table cut it off, so one big meadow can end up as several separate fields. A meeple lying down in a field is a farmer.

    During the gameNothing. Fields are never scored before the end, and farmers stay where they are.

    At the endTake one field with farmers at a time. Find every finished city whose walls touch that field. The player with the most farmers in that field gets 3 points for each of those cities. Unfinished cities give fields nothing.

    Where to look: the farmers lying in the grass, then walk around the edge of their field and look at every city wall it touches.

    Example: your farmer's field touches 3 finished cities and 1 unfinished one. That's 3 × 3 = 9 points; the unfinished city doesn't count.

    Every field is scored on its own. If one city touches two different fields, it counts for both of them. And if the same player wins both fields, that city earns them 3 points twice.

    A handy way to find a field's cities: put a finger on the farmer and trace the grass outwards without crossing any road or wall. Every finished city wall your finger runs along counts once for this field, no matter how long the wall is.

    Common mistakes

    Mistakes people make with Carcassonne scoring
    • Scoring unfinished cities like finished ones at the end. A finished city tile is worth 2, an unfinished one only 1, and the same goes for shields.
    • Forgetting the monastery tile itself. An unfinished monastery with 5 neighbours is worth 6, not 5.
    • Counting unfinished cities for fields. Fields only get points for finished cities.
    • Splitting the points in a tie. Every player tied for the most meeples gets the full points.
    • Giving points to the player with fewer meeples. Only the player or players with the most get anything.
    • Scoring fields during the game, or taking farmers back. Farmers stay until the very end.
    • Forgetting to take meeples back after a feature is scored during the game.
    • Counting the city's wall pieces instead of its tiles. Count each tile once, however much city is on it.

    Who wins

    Your final score is everything you collected on the scoring track during the game, plus the unfinished features and the fields from the end. The player with the most points wins. If two players have the same total, they share the victory. The track is shorter than most final scores, so if your marker went around it, don't forget to add the laps.

    An end of game, counted

    The last tile has just been placed, and your marker on the track shows that you collected 64 points during the game. Here's what's still on the table:

    1. An unfinished city of 3 tiles with 1 shield, where you have the only meeple: 3 + 1 = 4 points.
    2. An unfinished road over 2 tiles with your meeple: 2 points.
    3. An unfinished monastery with 5 tiles around it: 1 + 5 = 6 points.
    4. Your first field, where your farmer is alone, touches 3 finished cities: 3 × 3 = 9 points.
    5. Your second field is shared with Anna, 1 farmer each, and touches 2 finished cities. You're tied, so you both get 2 × 3 = 6 points.
    64 + 4 + 2 + 6 + 9 + 6 = 91 points

    Common questions

    Do I score a feature if I don't have a meeple on it?

    No. Only the players with the most meeples on a feature score it. A finished city without any meeples gives nobody points, though it can still help the farmers in the fields next to it at the end.

    What if I finish a feature and put a meeple on it in the same turn?

    It's scored right away, and the meeple comes back to you in the same turn.

    Do we split the points when we're tied?

    No. Every player tied for the most meeples gets the full points.

    Can one city give points to two fields?

    Yes. Each field is scored on its own, so a city touching several fields counts once for each of them.

    Do farmers ever come back?

    No. A farmer stays in its field until the end of the game, so think twice before laying one down early.

    Does a 2-tile city score less?

    No, in the current rules it's 2 points per tile like any other city, so 4 points when finished. Older printings of the rules differ in some details; if you play by an old rulebook, follow it.

    What about the expansions?

    This guide covers the base game only; expansions such as Inns & Cathedrals or the Abbot add new pieces and change some scores, and we'll explain them on separate pages.
